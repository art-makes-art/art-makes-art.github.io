### Underglaze on ceramic | 4" diameter x 8" height

This piece, whose Italian title translates to _You Are Not Made For Human Eyes_ or, perhaps more accurately, _You Are Not Made For The Eyes Of Men_ is a tribute to Alice Rohrwacher's 2023 film _La Chimera_. 

In the film (_spoiler alert!_), one of the central plot lines involves the protagonist, Arthur, and his group of grave-robbing friends discovering a beautiful, life-sized ancient statue of a woman, a goddess, in a previously undisturbed tomb. The group bluntly [separates the goddess' head from her body](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt14561712/mediaviewer/rm3258481409/?ref_=ttmi_mi_15_1), in attempt to carry the statue out of the tomb in pieces. Arthur is faced with a moral dilemma: to turn the head over to the large-scale curators who would pay an _inestimable_ amount for this priceless chunk of stone, or to return that which belongs to the dead, back to the earth? (_even bigger spoiler alert!_) Ultimately, he [looks into the goddess' eyes](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt14561712/mediaviewer/rm3241704193/?ref_=ttmi_mi_14_2), utters the words _'tu non sei fatta per gli occhi degli uomini,'_ and tosses the invaluable artifact into the sea.

The film invites us to be curious about the law and who it benefits/protects, ethics, money, greed, possession(s), power, poverty, survival, sacredness, gender, love, grief, fate, fear, and the living [memory] of the dead. This series of articles discusses the film and its commentary on (or questioning of) the ethics of archaeology in greater detail.

Throughout 2024, I was working at a pottery studio. One day, we received a shipment of ceramic _Día De (Los) Muertos_ pieces. Two of the statuettes had been broken in transit, arriving with their heads detached. As employees, we had free reign to paint any already-broken pieces for ourselves. I had just seen _La Chimera_ for the first time, so I was naturally inspired by this serendipitous headless statue, and knew I needed to find a way to honor the film and its messages.

On this piece, I hand-painted two stills ([left](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt14561712/mediaviewer/rm3907419905/?ref_=ttmi_mi_7_1) and [right](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt14561712/mediaviewer/rm3957751553/)) from the movie's opening credits. Another key metaphor or recurring motif in the film is a [red string](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt14561712/mediaviewer/rm3627906817/?ref_=ttmi_mi_30_3), symbolizing the pull of death, the dead, the ones we've loved and lost and our own inevitable and advancing call to join them. So, I decided to wrap those Italian words (_'tu non sei fatta per gli occhi degli uomini,'_) around the body of the piece in red ribbony cursive, coming from the statue's own hands.
