### Acrylic on denim | 27" x 25"

The world is on fire and so are our movements. From Black/queer/trans liberation to a Free Palestine to disability-centered anti-viral, anti-eugenics activism, our struggles are alive and inextricably connected. Most of my artistic drive and inspiration these days comes from the work and lives of queer elders, people with AIDS, who fought for recognition, respect, and survival in the face of systemic neglect, stigma, and abandonment. The same social, political, economic, and cultural forces that have conspired to kill millions of people with HIV and AIDS globally are now also responsible for killing millions of people with COVID. Courageous members of ACT UP (AIDS Coalition To Unleash Power) knew that silence = death and ignorance = fear. They knew that the violent status quo must not continue. It must be challenged, fought, toppled.\\ 

I painted this jacket in 2024 and I still stand by its meanings and messages today, as we enter Year 8 of the global SARS-CoV-2 pandemic. The colorful faces lining the back/bottom of the jacket are a tribute to the album art for Nobody Is Listening by artist ZAYN (Zayn Malik). The album holds personal significance for me, and the messages hit painfully close to home for those of us still trying to reject the violent status quo (“back to normal”) of COVID denial and minimization. Disabled/covid-informed/anti-eugenics activists and people with Long COVID are scapegoated, dismissed as paranoid fear-mongerers. Our experiences, wisdoms, and warnings are disregarded, no matter how politely we aim to break the news of such large-scale, daily propaganda and violences. As activists and oppressed people throughout history and throughout the world know all too well, we are left feeling (made to believe) that, in fact, nobody is listening. However, as the last refrain on ZAYN’s album goes, “Don’t you ever hope for something else?”\\

I will point back to the word coalition. Our movements, our futures, our survival, our communities, are stronger when we maintain an interconnected lens, knowing that the struggle for Black lives is not separate from the struggle for a Free Palestine is not separate from the struggle for disabled survival in the face of COVID denial. There is so much to learn about how we can fight this ongoing pandemic, how we can resist our own demanded deaths, if only we are willing to listen. If only we are willing to face our mortality. If only we are willing to read the words and understand the messages of people who were killed by government neglect. If only we are willing to associate ourselves with the diseased, the dirty, the sinful, the pathetic, the crazy. If only we are willing to admit that the terrors of the past are with us now more than ever, merely in shapeshifted forms, in different fonts, in more subtle packaging. If only we are willing to admit we were wrong.\\
