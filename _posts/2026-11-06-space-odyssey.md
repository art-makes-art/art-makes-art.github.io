### Acrylic on canvas | 20" x 20"


This piece is representative of my signature style as an artist, favoring discrete shapes and bright, contrasting colors. My painting method began in 2018. I was 18 years old, struggling to survive amidst the traumas, turmoils, lack of freedoms, and alienation of [a gendered, neuroqueer] adolescence. In January of my senior year of high school, I began painting. I drew an 11 x 11 grid on a 10” x 10” canvas. I painted one square per day, and it served as my countdown until graduation. 121 squares for 121 days. It felt bleak at the time. Scrambling for branches to keep me from being pulled under by the rushing current. Keeping my eye on the light at the end of the seemingly never-ending hellish tunnel around me. It was a coping mechanism. But it was also a statement of hope, an affirmation of belief in myself. Better times were coming. Colorful times. If only I could make it through this chapter, this canvas, the world would open up. I would be able to breathe, to relax, to collapse. I could finally become whoever I felt so strongly I needed to become, once I was away from this place. I wasn’t sure who that future self was, or why I felt so bad, or why I was so drawn to the (queer as hell) college towns I’d visited. All I was sure of was one day at a time, one square at a time.

I picked up this painting practice once again in January 2023, this time after I’d made the decision to leave my graduate astrophysics PhD program early with my “only” Master’s. Another countdown to graduation, to a freedom of sorts. This time, I upgraded my canvas. I drew an 18 x 18 grid on a 20” x 20” canvas (don’t ask why I can never just stick with the optimal Square Inch). 324 squares for 324 days. It felt less bleak this time. I’d received my autism diagnosis at the end of 2022 and with it, I suppose, I gave myself renewed permission to give up on the exhausting, ill-fitting roles and facades I’d been flailing to maintain my whole life. Quitting grad school felt like choosing myself, finally, for one of the first times in my life. It felt like embracing my autonomy and asserting my authority over my own life. But still, I wasn’t sure what I was counting down towards. I had no job prospects lined up. No income. Few deep relationships and fewer certainties about the future. I painted my 324th square on December 21, 2023. Two months later, I was working at a pottery studio. Six months later, I’d started HRT. Perhaps this is what I’d been waiting for, counting down towards?

So, no, this piece is not some lovey-dovey-generic-Hobby-Lobby inspirational message about “making every day count” or “daily blessings.” It’s about survival. In particular, surviving through periods of intense loneliness as an isolated and/or closeted (unrealized, egg, repressed, whatever) LGBTQIA+ disabled person. Surviving while knowing that there is more. There can be more. There must be more. And even if I cannot know it fully or see it fully or live it fully now, day-by-day, I will create it. Throughout and especially in the aftermath of the making of this painting, I have found my way towards (and took part in creating) the kinds of love, community, and friendship I had all but given up on. I have re-grounded myself in my values. I have made so much art, written so many poems, made so many more goals and plans than I could ever achieve in a human lifetime. But I have remembered how to dream. The reassertion of one’s self opens the door for more authentic, mutual relationships of care, solidarity, and love with others, too. And I hope that my 18-year-old self would be proud to see just what all those squares have added up to.
